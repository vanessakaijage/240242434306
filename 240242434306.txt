I have learned about data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns from large datasets, and that it is the core step of the KDD process: data cleaning, integration, selection, transformation, data mining, pattern evaluation, and knowledge presentation. Data objects are described by attributes, which can be nominal, binary, ordinal, or numeric (interval or ratio), and we study their center, spread, and relationships to detect noise and outliers. The main focus was measuring similarity and dissimilarity, which is used in clustering, outlier detection, and nearest-neighbor classification. For nominal data, dissimilarity is (p - m) / p, where p is the number of attributes and m is the number of matches; for binary data we use a contingency table and the Jaccard coefficient, (r + s) / (q + r + s); for numeric data we use Euclidean, Manhattan, Minkowski, and Supremum distances; for ordinal data we convert ranks to a 0-1 scale using (r - 1) / (M - 1) and treat them as numeric; and for mixed data we combine per-attribute dissimilarities into one score. I also learned about cosine similarity.
